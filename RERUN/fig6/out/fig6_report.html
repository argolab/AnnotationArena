What changed

Two corrections · one extension

1. The evaluation set (D22/D23). test.py builds train_obs + test_obs + test_miss + train_miss and averages every missing token, while Stan is scored on missing_ratings_indexes_in_test_instance alone. The paper says the corner is the target: “All reported log-losses are evaluated on missing entries within this corner.” At K=4000 only 2.9% of the published Marformer number is corner.

2. Checkpoint selection (D14). Published points are an argmin over ~18 checkpoints scored on test. These use block-validation: carve, select the stopping epoch on the carve, refit on the whole training partition. Test is never consulted and the refit’s data budget equals Stan’s.

3. The NT curve now spans the full sweep — it stopped at K=900, and the plot script capped it at 500, while the transductive arm ran to 4000.

On the Stan curves. No Domain 3 sigmoid Stan results survived the migration, so these are read by eye off the published figures rather than recomputed — roughly ±0.005 nats. They need no correction: Stan was always scored on the corner, which is why overlaying them here is apples-to-apples and the published figure was not. Beyond K=500 on the item axis the paper draws Stan flat for cost reasons; that is reproduced as a dotted reference, not a measurement.

Three things to know before quoting these

Caveats that change how the panels should be read

  • The error bars understate the uncertainty (D27). The bands are the standard deviation across 3 seeds (~±0.01). The corner is a fixed 414 cells, so any measurement carries about ±0.06 at 95% from cell sampling, and more seeds cannot reduce it because every seed scores the same cells. Point-to-point moves under 0.06 are not signal. This is a deliberate paired design — a fixed hard corner lowers comparison variance — but the reported error bar should be a bootstrap over cells, not a seed sd.
  • The spike at K=600 is a chunking artifact (D24), not the model. It lands exactly where the graph first needs a second chunk at max_item=300. Raising the chunk size to 500 moved it to K=900 rather than removing it. The actual fix is to keep the corner whole and in a chunk that carries training context, which is what these panels now do — it removes the spike and the apparent NT divergence together.
  • CORRECTED — the non-transductive arm does not diverge. An earlier version of this page reported NT degrading badly past K=900 (1.13 at K=4000). That was an evaluation artifact: the corner is always the highest item ids, so contiguous chunking put 239 of its 414 cells in a 36-item orphan chunk containing zero training items, where they scored 1.29 against 0.86 for the cells that kept context. Evaluating with the corner held in one chunk with training context, NT tracks the transductive arm within ±0.03 at every size (0.8673 vs 0.8616 at K=4000). The same fix removes the K=600 spike in the transductive curve (1.0934 → 0.9207); both were the same root cause. The paper’s claim that NT is “comparable at large training sizes” holds.
